# Product and Business Brief

## Problem
Users know what kind of evening they want but conventional place directories are organized around venue names, categories, photos and generic ratings. They do not consistently describe *what an outing will feel like on a particular date*. Event announcements are fragmented across venue pages and social channels.

## Value proposition
**Where to Go matches the right people with the right experience at the right time.** Explain not only what a venue is but why a specific night fits a user's preferences.

## Core example
“Thursday evening, four friends in their 40s, live Arabic music, conversation-friendly seating, outdoor preference, AED 150–300 per person.” Return a small ranked shortlist with verified event schedules, trade-offs, expected spending, freshness and reservation handoffs. Age is an optional preference signal, not an assertion about actual audience demographics or a discriminatory filter.

## Target and launch
Pilot in Dubai with friends and small groups seeking live music, jazz, Arabic performances and other atmosphere-led evenings. Expansion to families, daytime activities, other cities and broader events follows validation.

## User jobs
Discover; compare atmosphere; plan by date; agree with companions; save/follow interests; reserve externally; provide post-visit corrections.

## Venue jobs
Claim and manage a profile; post recurring and one-off performances; verify price bands and venue characteristics; see interest and referral metrics; correct inaccurate information.

## Competitive thesis to test
Generic maps, restaurant platforms, event marketplaces, social networks and AI assistants are substitutes. No assertion of uncontested market or competitor feature absence should be made without evidence. Differentiate on structured, time-indexed atmosphere data, verification, matching and venue relationships.

## Business model hypothesis
Keep base listing free. Explore paid enhanced profiles, operator insights and disclosed sponsorship; referral fees only through lawful partnerships. Sponsored status must never bypass user hard constraints or masquerade as organic ranking.

## North-star metric
**Verified satisfactory outings facilitated** (initially proxy through qualified outbound booking clicks plus opt-in post-visit confirmation; avoid treating clicks as completed bookings).

## Pilot measures and targets to validate
- Data coverage: 50–100 manually curated venues.
- Verified scheduled event accuracy: pilot target >=80% on a sampled audit.
- Top-three relevance: user testing target >=70% finding one acceptable option.
- Repeat usage: track four-week cohort return, no invented benchmark.
- Operator engagement: percent of claimed venues updating weekly.
- Search outcomes: searches with at least one eligible, source-backed result; zero-result rate.
- Commercial: outbound booking conversion proxies, operator willingness to pay.

## Risks
Data freshness; legal rights for third-party content; thin event supply; venue update burden; biased demographic claims; AI hallucination; costs of manual curation; weak retention; platform distribution and acquisition costs.

## Decisions requiring founder approval
Brand/domain; Arabic at launch or after English MVP; target neighborhoods; whether alcohol-related venue metadata is shown; initial content partners; moderation policy; preferred maps provider; budget and hosting jurisdiction; booking partners; commercial launch timing.
